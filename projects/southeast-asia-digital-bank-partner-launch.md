# Launching a Southeast Asian Digital Bank Through Accountable Partners

I completed this work during my [McKinsey experience from July 2014 to June 2016](https://github.com/beastofbayarea/shivam-singh-partner-gtm/blob/main/shivam-singh-partner-gtm.pdf).

The opportunity was not simply to distribute a banking app. The target population included many people without formal credit histories, and the product depended on telco signals, identity verification, payment access, servicing, and regulatory approval. Every partner could accelerate reach; every partner could also introduce a break in consent, evidence, underwriting, or customer support.

I built the launch around partners as accountable operating participants rather than acquisition channels.

## The ecosystem followed the customer journey

I mapped responsibility from discovery through eKYC and liveness, consented alternative-data input, underwriting, account activation, payment use, servicing, collections, complaints, and model monitoring.

For each handoff, the operating model identified the data provided, permitted purpose, quality threshold, response time, exception owner, customer communication, and evidence retained. More than 400 behavioral features were converted into partner requirements and controls that a distribution or data provider could actually operate.

FATF's risk-based guidance for mobile and internet payments shaped the allocation of identity and monitoring effort. The goal was not identical friction for every customer; it was stronger control where the observed risk required it.

## Market access and product utility were connected

The World Bank's 2014 Global Findex supplied the contemporaneous evidence on account access and digital-payment opportunity. The BIS and World Bank work on payment aspects of financial inclusion reinforced that a useful transaction account, identification, interoperability, and broad access all matter.

I therefore measured partners on activated, successfully served customers—not registrations delivered. Channel economics tracked acquisition cost alongside approval, payment use, support, and credit quality.

## Launch readiness was a shared gate

Partner onboarding covered data quality, identity evidence, consent, credit-policy implementation, operational support, complaint handling, and measurable activation. Product, Risk, Compliance, Data Science, and channel owners reviewed the same launch record.

The Monetary Authority of Singapore's June 2016 sandbox consultation provided a contemporaneous model for bounded experimentation. I used that logic to define the initial customer boundary, safeguards, measures, reporting, and exit conditions.

Expansion proceeded only when approval, non-performing loans, acquisition cost, identity quality, servicing, and partner readiness remained inside the agreed thresholds.

## What the ecosystem delivered

- The launch reached 100,000 active users in 90 days.
- Approval increased from 10% to 40%.
- Non-performing loans remained at 2.4%.
- Customer-acquisition cost stayed below $10.

Those measures had to move together. A partner that increased approvals while damaging identity quality or loss performance did not create healthy scale.

## The partner lesson

In regulated distribution, reach is the beginning of the proposition. The partner must also protect consent, deliver reliable evidence, support the customer, and accept operating accountability. I design the commercial incentive and the control boundary around the same completed customer outcome.
