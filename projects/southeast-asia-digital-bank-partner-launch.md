# Making every partner accountable for a digital-bank customer

A channel found the customer. An identity vendor established who they were. A telco supplied consented behavior. A model assessed risk. Payments made the account useful. Servicing and collections owned what happened afterward.

Without a shared operating contract, every partner could succeed locally while the customer failed end to end.

During my July 2014–June 2016 McKinsey role, I made the partner chain operate as one model across bank leaders, regulators, Product, Risk, Compliance/Data, Identity/Telco, payment networks, and distribution.

## The same contract followed every handoff

Each partner interface specified:

**purpose | minimum data | consent evidence | quality | response time | exception owner | customer communication | audit evidence | stop condition**

The complete journey was:

**discovery → eKYC/liveness → consent → derived features → underwriting → activation → first payment → servicing → collections → complaint → monitoring**

More than 400 candidate features became partner-operable rules: allowed sources, transformations, freshness, missingness, correction, and disputed-decision evidence. A distribution agreement was never blanket permission to collect everything.

## Four partner economics replaced one signup target

### Identity

Measure completion, manual review, confirmed fraud, false rejection, response time, unresolved exceptions. Completion could not improve by weakening proof.

### Telco/data

Measure consent, provenance, completeness, latency, stability, correction, drift. Share derived signals for the underwriting purpose, not raw data by default.

### Super-app/commerce distribution

Pay for active, safely served customers—not registrations. The funnel continued through identity, approval, activation, transactions, and retention, with CAC beside complaints and credit quality.

### Payments/servicing

An approved account unable to make an everyday payment did not count as inclusion. Measure successful use, failures, availability, resolution, and complaint ownership. Collections entered before launch so approval growth could not externalize harm.

The [CPMI–World Bank 2016 report](https://www.bis.org/cpmi/publ/d144.htm) emphasized transaction-account adoption and use. [FATF’s 2013 mobile-payment guidance](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/dam/fatf-gafi/guidance/Guidance-RBA-NPPS.pdf.coredownload.pdf) supported proportional, risk-based controls—not reduced accountability.

## Expansion was earned

One launch record joined Product, Risk, Compliance, Data Science, Operations, and partner owners: identity quality, approval, activation, payment use, CAC, NPL, complaints, incidents, and drift.

Population and exposure began bounded. Expansion required all critical measures inside limits and exceptions owned.

The source calls this a regulatory sandbox. Singapore’s MAS consultation appeared only in June 2016 and does not prove participation. I preserve the bounded-experiment design without inventing regulator or license.

## Launch result

| Customer state | Baseline → target → recorded result | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Active use | 0 → prove real use → 100,000 in 90 days | Exact activity event absent |
| Approval | <10% → widen within loss limit → >40% | Approved / eligible; eligibility definition needed |
| Early credit quality | no seasoned vintage → within appetite → 2.4% NPL | DPD definition and seasoning absent |
| Acquisition economics | no launch baseline → <$10 → <$10 | Spend / acquired customer; active-vs-registered denominator absent |

Approval and NPL travel together. >4× approval with 2.4% early NPL supported the launch boundary; it was not lifetime loss evidence. Likewise, 100,000 users proves scale only when “active” remains consistent.

Customer-spanning contracts, feature-to-partner translation, the four scorecards, incentive alignment, bounded expansion, and the shared executive record were my deliverables. The bank retained Product/Risk authority; regulators approval; specialist partners their controls.

The conflict was structural: distribution wanted reach, modeling wanted more data, Compliance wanted restraint, Risk wanted seasoning. I did not average them. I made every partner’s reward depend on the same governed customer state and made expansion conditional on the joint record.
